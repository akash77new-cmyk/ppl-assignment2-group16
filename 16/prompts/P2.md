## Unit 1 — expression trees

> Write only the expression-tree generator for `16_q2.pl`. Represent a tree as
> `n(Integer)` for a leaf and `e(Op, Left, Right)` for an operator node.
> `expr_tree(Numbers, Tree)` must produce every expression over exactly
> `Numbers` in the given order, using `+ - * /`. Nothing else yet.

**Returned:** a two-clause generator — one-element list is a leaf; otherwise
`append/3` splits into a non-empty prefix and suffix and an operator joins the
two sides.

**Accepted.** The `Front = [_|_], Back = [_|_]` guards are what make lists of
length 0 and 1 fail rather than loop, which P2.a requires.

## Unit 2 — evaluation with exact division

> Write `eval(Tree, Value)`. Integer arithmetic only. Division must succeed
> only when the divisor is non-zero and the division is exact, and that has to
> hold for every division inside the tree, not just the outermost one.

**Returned, first attempt:** used `V is A / B` with a check that the result is
an integer.

**REJECTED.** In SWI `/` can yield a float or a rational depending on the
`prefer_rationals` flag, so the test is flag-dependent rather than exact.
Flagged in the prolog-dev skill as failure mode 3.

> Do it without relying on `/` at all — guard the exactness arithmetically and
> use integer division.

**Returned:**

```prolog
eval(e(/, L, R), V) :-
    eval(L, A), eval(R, B),
    B =\= 0,
    A rem B =:= 0,
    V is A // B.
```

**Accepted.** `rem` gives the sign-safe remainder test, and once the remainder
is zero `//` is exact regardless of the rounding flag. Confirmed by
`all_equations([7,2,3], Es)` giving `[]` (7/2 is not exact) and
`all_equations([1,0,0], Es)` returning normally rather than raising.

## Unit 3 — canonical printing

> Write `tree_string(Tree, String)` implementing the assignment's
> parenthesisation rule exactly: `*` and `/` bind tighter than `+` and `-`;
> a left operand is parenthesised only when its precedence is strictly lower
> than its parent's; a right operand when its precedence is lower than or
> equal to its parent's; a number is never parenthesised. Result must be an
> SWI string, no spaces.

**Returned:** `op_prec/2`, `node_prec/2` with numbers given precedence 3 so
they are never wrapped, and `tree_string/2` using `string_concat/3`.

**Accepted** after checking the rule against the worked examples in the
assignment, one at a time: `8/4*2` prints bare (left operand, equal
precedence, `2 < 2` false), `8/(4*2)` is wrapped (right operand, equal
precedence, `2 =< 2` true), `(4+2)*3` is wrapped, `4+2*3` is not, and
`1+(2+3)` and `1+2+3` print differently — which is what makes them count as
two distinct equations.

## Unit 4 — assembling equations and the public predicates

> Write `equation/2`, then `generate_equation/2`, `all_equations/2` and
> `count_equations/2` on top of it. The split point must leave both sides
> non-empty. `all_equations` is sorted by `sort/2` with duplicates removed.

**Returned:** `equation/2` splitting with `append/3` and requiring both sides
to evaluate to the same value, `generate_equation/2` as `once(equation(...))`,
`all_equations/2` as `findall/3` followed by `sort/2`.

**Accepted.** `once/1` at the call site rather than a `!` inside `equation/2`,
so `equation/2` stays usable as a generator — and `generate_equation/2` still
works when its second argument is already bound, which was checked.
