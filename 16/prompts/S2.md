## Unit 3 — `make-inserter`

> Write the higher-order `(make-inserter x len-ok?)`. It returns a function of
> one list that inserts `x` at the front of every list, at every level of
> nesting, whose length satisfies `len-ok?`. Two things to be careful about:
> the length is the length of the ORIGINAL list, measured before any
> insertion; and `'()` is a list of length 0, not an atom, so it must be
> handled as a list.

**Returned:** a `letrec` with a `walk` lambda and a three-way `cond` — empty
list, non-empty list, atom — measuring the original length *before* recursing
into the elements.

**Accepted.** The two traps named in the prompt are both real: testing
`(pair? item)` alone would treat `'()` as an atom and never insert into it,
and measuring after the recursive walk would use the post-insertion length of
the children. Both are in the scheme-dev skill as failure modes 4 and 5.

**Accepted.** `let*` rather than `let` — the three `read` calls must happen in
